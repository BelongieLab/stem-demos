What happens when potential energy becomes elasticity?

Scroll through the step-by-step mathematical breakdown below. Watch the vector dynamics, harmonic forces, and phase space trajectories adapt dynamically in real-time.

STAGE 01 Freefall ($y > 0$)

Gravitational Acceleration

Before making contact with the trampoline mat, the object of mass $m$ is in pure freefall under constant gravitational acceleration $g$.

$$F_{net} = -mg$$
$$v(t) = -gt = -\sqrt{2g(h - y)}$$
$$E_{total} = E_g = mgh$$

As the ball falls from height $h$, potential energy $E_g$ converts entirely into kinetic energy $E_k = \frac{1}{2}m v_{impact}^2$ right before impact.

STAGE 02 Touchdown ($y \le 0$)

Hooke's Law & Restoring Force

Upon touching the trampoline surface ($y = 0$), the mat exerts a restoring force proportional to the downward displacement $y$. This is modeled by Hooke's Law:

$$F_{spring} = -k y \quad (y \le 0)$$
$$F_{net} = -mg - ky$$

Notice that gravity pulls downward while the mat pushes upward. The net force flips upward once displacement $y < -mg / k$.

STAGE 03 Equilibrium & Peak Acceleration

Equilibrium vs. Maximum Depression

There are two critical vertical positions during contact:

  • Equilibrium Position ($y_{eq}$): Where $F_{net} = 0 \implies y_{eq} = -\frac{mg}{k}$. Speed $v$ is maximum here!
  • Maximum Compression ($y_{min}$): Where speed drops to zero ($v = 0$), storing maximum elastic potential energy $U_e$.
$$mgh = mgy_{min} + \frac{1}{2}k y_{min}^2$$
$$\implies \frac{1}{2}k y_{min}^2 + mg y_{min} - mgh = 0$$
$$y_{min} = \frac{-mg - \sqrt{(mg)^2 + 2kmgh}}{k}$$
STAGE 04 Energy Conservation

The Continuous Energy Exchange

Without friction or air resistance, total mechanical energy $E_{total}$ is strictly conserved throughout the bounce cycle:

$$E_{total} = E_k + E_g + E_e = \text{Constant}$$
$$\frac{1}{2}m v^2 + mgy + \frac{1}{2}k (\min(0, y))^2 = mgh$$

Observe how potential energy transforms from pure gravity (yellow) into kinetic (green), and finally into elastic potential energy (cyan).

STAGE 05 Phase Space Portrait

Phase Space & Real-World Damping

In phase space—plotting velocity $v$ against position $y$—an ideal bounce forms a closed loop. Introducing damping ($-\gamma v$) turns this trajectory into a spiral converging toward the static equilibrium point $y_{eq}$.

$$m \ddot{y} + \gamma \dot{y} + k y \cdot \mathbb{I}_{y \le 0} = -mg$$
