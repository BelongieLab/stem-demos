The Endless Fetch

Meet Zeno. He loves math.

Meet his dog, Barnaby. Barnaby loves fetching the red ball.

Scroll down to play ⬇️

Zeno throws the ball.

Barnaby waits patiently. Now, Zeno wants to walk over and pet Barnaby.

Sounds easy, right? Just walk to the dog.

But Zeno's brain starts thinking like an ancient Greek philosopher...

"Before I can reach Barnaby," Zeno thinks, "I first have to walk halfway there."

Okay, he made it halfway. But look!

There's still distance left. Before he can cross that remaining distance, he has to cross half of THAT.

That's 1/2 of 1/2, which is 1/4 of the total trip.

He steps forward again.

"Wait," Zeno says, stopping again. "I still have a little bit to go. I must cover half of this new remaining distance first!"

Now he's adding 1/8 to his journey.

Zeno takes a tiny step.

"And half of THAT..." (+ 1/16)

A teeny-tiny step.

"And half of THAT..." (+ 1/32)

Barnaby whimpers. 🐕

The Paradox!

Zeno realizes a terrible mathematical truth.

Because he can always divide the remaining distance in half, there is an infinite number of halves to cross.

"I can NEVER reach my dog!"

Does it add up?

Let's look at all those fractions being added together:

If you have an infinite number of steps, shouldn't it take an infinite amount of time?

Calculus to the Rescue!

Zeno lived over 2,000 years ago. It took a long time, but mathematicians eventually figured it out using Limits.

Even though there are an infinite number of steps, the time it takes to do each step gets infinitely smaller!

The infinite sum exactly equals 1 full trip.

Zeno finally takes the last (infinitely small) step.

"Good boy, Barnaby!" 🎾
